ISPM 15 — The Global Standard for Export Wooden Packaging

ISPM 15 (International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15) is a global regulation established by the International Plant Protection Convention (IPPC) under the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). It governs all wooden packaging material (WPM) used in international trade — including pallets, crates, boxes, dunnage, and skids.

The regulation was introduced to prevent the international spread of invasive insects and plant diseases that can live inside raw timber. Non-compliant wooden packaging is routinely seized and destroyed at international ports — causing massive delays, financial losses, and supply chain disruption.

The Stakes

Why ISPM 15 Compliance is Non-Negotiable for Exporters

Customs Seizure

Non-compliant wooden packaging is confiscated at the border. Your cargo cannot proceed until the packaging is replaced or destroyed on-site.

Financial Loss

Demurrage charges, re-packaging costs, lost contracts, and damaged relationships with overseas buyers. The financial impact can be severe.

Shipping Delays

Vessels sail without your cargo. Containers sit at port. Production lines shut down at the destination. Every day of delay has a cost.

Contract Penalties

Export contracts often include delivery date guarantees. Compliance failures that cause delays can trigger penalty clauses and damage your reputation.

How It's Done

ISPM 15 Treatment Methods

Two approved treatment methods — we offer both, with Heat Treatment as our primary method.

Heat Treatment (HT)

Our Primary Method

The timber is heated in a registered kiln until the core temperature reaches a minimum of 56°C and is maintained for at least 30 continuous minutes. This kills all insects, larvae, and eggs without using any chemicals.

  • No chemical residue
  • Environmentally preferred method
  • Accepted in all ISPM 15 countries
  • Suitable for food-adjacent cargo
  • Quick process — 4 to 8 hours per batch
  • Official IPPC mark: HT
IPPC Mark: XX / INDIA / 00 / HT

Methyl Bromide Fumigation (MB)

The timber or finished packaging is placed in a sealed chamber and treated with methyl bromide gas to kill all live organisms.

  • Alternative to heat treatment
  • Accepted in most ISPM 15 countries
  • Being phased out (banned in some regions)
  • Not accepted in Canada, Mexico, China
  • Chemical treatment — restrictions for food
  • Official IPPC mark: MB
IPPC Mark: XX / INDIA / 00 / MB

Our Recommendation: Heat Treatment (HT) is the preferred method globally — chemical-free, broadly accepted, and increasingly mandated by importing countries.

The Official Mark

Understanding the IPPC Mark

Every ISPM 15 compliant piece of wooden packaging must carry the official IPPC mark (the wheat stalk symbol). The mark contains specific encoded information about where the packaging was produced and treated.

Common Questions

ISPM 15 FAQ

ISPM 15 applies to all wooden packaging material (WPM) used in international trade, including pallets, crates, boxes, dunnage, skids, and load boards. It does not apply to processed wood products like plywood, particle board, or MDF — as the manufacturing process eliminates pest risk. However, unprocessed timber and sawn wood in packaging must comply.

Over 180 countries worldwide have adopted ISPM 15 as a mandatory import requirement. This includes all major trading nations: the USA, EU member states, UK, Canada, Australia, China, Japan, UAE, and most of Southeast Asia and Africa. If you are shipping internationally, you should assume ISPM 15 compliance is required unless you have specific confirmation otherwise.

Once treated and marked, the compliance is permanent — the mark remains valid as long as the packaging is not repaired with untreated wood. If the packaging is significantly repaired or modified using new wood, the new wood must also be treated and the packaging re-marked. These documents support your Phytosanitary Certificate application if required by the destination country.

ISPM 15 is strictly an international trade requirement. For domestic shipments within India, there is no legal mandate to use ISPM 15 certified packaging. However, some clients choose to use it for domestic shipments to maintain consistent packaging standards across their supply chain.
