### What Is ISPM 15?
- **H2:** What Is ISPM 15?
- ISPM 15 is the international rule for wooden packaging used in export. Pallets, boxes and crates must be
  treated to kill pests and stamped with the IPPC mark — otherwise the shipment can be held or sent back at
  the destination port.
- We heat-treat our solar pallets and box pallets for export orders, so your shipment leaves with packaging
  that meets the standard. ⚠️
- Fact tiles: **56 °C** core temperature · **30 min** minimum hold · **HT** heat only, no chemicals ·
  **IPPC** mark on every unit

### Our Treatment Process (4 steps)
1. **Build** — Pallets and boxes are made from debarked timber.
2. **Heat** — Loaded into the heat chamber until the wood core reaches 56 °C.
3. **Hold** — Held at temperature for at least 30 minutes, with the run logged.
4. **Mark** — Stamped with the IPPC mark and dispatched with the treatment record.

### Reading the IPPC Mark
- **H2:** Reading the IPPC Mark
- Diagram: IPPC symbol | IN-000 | HT
- **IN** — Country code (India)
- **000** — Producer number issued by India's plant protection authority (NPPO)
- **HT** — Treatment type: heat treatment
- Note: The mark is stamped or burned into the wood — never a sticker.

### FAQ
- **Do I need ISPM 15 for shipments within India?** — No. ISPM 15 applies to international shipments.
  Some customers still use treated pallets everywhere to keep one specification.
- **Does plywood need ISPM 15?** — No. Plywood and other processed wood are exempt. Solid timber pallets and
  boxes need treatment for export.
- **Does the treatment expire?** — No. If a pallet is repaired with new wood, that wood must be treated and
  the pallet re-marked.
- **What documents do I get?** — Treated orders come with a heat treatment record for your shipping
  documents. ⚠️

### Where product photos come from (no AI images, no copied images)

AI-generated pictures don't show the products accurately, so they are dropped for product shots.
**Never copy photos from other companies' websites** (solar manufacturers, other pallet makers, EPC
firms): they are copyrighted, using them on a commercial site invites takedown notices, and showing
someone else's pallets as Erawat's is misleading. The only acceptable sources:

1. **The client's own photos** — best by far: real, free, and they build trust.
2. **A customer's photo of Erawat pallets in use**, with the customer's written permission (an email is enough).
3. **A paid, licensed stock photo**, bought under a standard commercial licence. Free libraries (Unsplash,
   Pexels, Pixabay, Wikimedia Commons) have nothing that shows solar pallets or box pallets; paid libraries
   do, e.g. [Adobe Stock — solar modules stacked on pallets at a PV park](https://stock.adobe.com/images/pallets-containing-solar-modules-and-components-for-solar-panels-at-the-construction-site-of-a-photovoltaic-park-solar-panels-stacked-on-pallets-for-installation-and-construction-waste-in-background/757891974),
   [Shutterstock — "pallet solar" search](https://www.shutterstock.com/search/pallet-solar),
   [Dreamstime — "solar pallet" search](https://www.dreamstime.com/photos-images/solar-pallet.html).
   Avoid listings marked "Generative AI".
